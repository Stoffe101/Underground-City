# Sprint report, October 24, 2026 (sixth pass): toward the First Fully Playable Sandbox Alpha

Branch `feature/world-playability-overhaul`, draft PR [#25](https://github.com/Stoffe101/Underground-City/pull/25). Owner brief: "Underground City: Sixth Major Pass — First Fully Playable Sandbox Alpha, Roadmap Reconciliation, Deep Progression & Living-World Integration". Decisions D-167 to D-174 in [decisions.md](decisions.md). The milestone and where each of its twelve exit criteria stands are in the [roadmap](roadmap.md#milestone-first-fully-playable-sandbox-alpha).

**Nobody has played any of this.** Everything below was run by scripts, Forge GameTests, the Python suite, the economic model and scripted real clients (software-rendered in a virtual display, driven by synthetic key and mouse events). Evidence levels are named as in the roadmap: L1 unit and data checks, L2 GameTests and headless runs, L3 one scripted real client, L4 two scripted real clients, L5 a person. They are never added together. **The alpha milestone is not proven: its last criterion is a person playing it.**

## What changed for a player

* **No more freeze arriving in a city (D-167).** The roughly 600 ms pause on the first street pass after arriving in a city was the server waiting on Lost Cities' plan lock while its world-generation workers built the plans of the chunks being generated round the player. Core now records each chunk's plan as Lost Cities generates it and answers from the record; periodic questions about an unrecorded chunk wait until generation is quiet. A second wait, the alarm panels reading a chunk still being generated, is gone too. See [lost-cities-stalls.md](../technical/lost-cities-stalls.md#the-city-arrival-hitch-core-waiting-on-lost-cities-plan-lock-october-24-d-167).
* **The Gunsmith builds real TaCZ firearms (D-168).** Fourteen Gun Smith Table recipes turn a Firearm Parts Kit (legal) or an Unmarked Parts Kit (underground), both from the Gunsmith's Bench, and a few ingots into TaCZ pistols, revolvers, shotguns and, unmarked, an Uzi, an MP5A5 and an AK-47. A kit build leaves the table with a serial from its city's register and the maker's name, or with no number at all, which a checkpoint treats as restricted. Two contracts ask for builds by provenance.
* **Trade premises (D-169).** Ironhaven's commercial workshop and warehouse make a workshop station inside them run one more batch a quarter faster for whoever may use them, and open three bulk orders. They cost what any home costs, on the household's active clock; a lapse withdraws the bonus and takes nothing. Eight of the ten property categories now have a building in a new furnished world.
* **Crew contracts (D-170).** A Crew takes on a contract of parts by career (the depot canteen; the Valewood harvest fair). Each member delivers their own part for their own pay; when the last part lands the bonus is split equally among those who delivered, paid once each, at once or at the next login. A supplier can fill a part nobody can make, at a stated price, earning nobody a share.
* **A civic project that changes the world (D-171).** The Valewood Market Council's contributions (one of them open to every career) reopen the market: two stalls with lanterns are built once on public ground near Valewood's centre, residents talk about it, and the council's better stall orders open.
* **Beginner burglary pays something (D-172).** A savings tin and silver cutlery turn up from the shabbiest homes; a tier 1 house is worth about 23 credits to a fence instead of 2.
* **The first raid (D-173).** Kessler in Blackwater hires a thief with Professional Tools (Thief level 8) to lift a seized shipment from Ironhaven's bonded store, behind the warehouse's own high-security lock (a Pro Pick Set), cameras and alarm and the police beat, after the yard's night trade closes at three. The order is tracked on the status display (and Xaero's Minimap): the store's door, then Blackwater. The shipment is stolen goods at any checkpoint on the way.
* **General skills that do something (D-174).** Trading rises with legal sales and makes the exchanges up to 5% cheaper (inside the 10% cap the career skills share); Social rises with work for the city and the Crew and lets legal buyers offer trusted work one step sooner.
* **Smaller things.** The registration card no longer promises Blackwater a harbour that does not exist. After dark, people stand outside the bars and at the works' door a while before going in.

## Not done, and why

* **A person.** Nobody has played any of it; the alpha's last exit criterion is a person's two two-hour sessions.
* **Two real clients on the new systems.** Only the Crew contract has run with two clients connected, and its deliveries are driven by the server in that journey. Trade premises, the civic project, the raid, the Gunsmith's builds and the general skills have one client at most.
* **An unmarked kit build through a real client** (the registered build passed, gunsmith run 6); the unmarked kit needs a tier 2 bench and the Unregistered Builds skill.
* **The Farmer's and Smith's workshop stations through a real client**, and the back-channel job through a client (the Vintner's station was rerun and the Gunsmith's Bench run, above).
* **The larger apartment and the premium residence** have no building; no property tax and no market between players.
* **No tax of any kind** (P4's and the MVP's missing item) and **no per-player loot** (P6's): both need an owner decision first.
* **Vehicles:** no fuel, repair, insurance, car dealer or harbour.
* **Population:** interiors, and a dedicated-server measurement with two players.
* **General skills:** no panel on the terminal (notices and `/uc skills` only); driving, fitness and shooting are still unearned.
* **Usable buildings:** two thirds of Lost Cities' own buildings are still sealed scenery; no audit of them was done in this pass.

## Bugs found and fixed in this pass

* The arrival hitch (above), and the alarm panels' blocking read found while measuring it.
* The registration card's harbour.
* The rifle-chain GameTest counted only the contracts' XP after the build at the table began paying its own (found by the firearms-pack run 1).
* **The raid could pick a warehouse nobody could enter** (raid run 1): 15% of Ironhaven's furnished warehouses are let as trade premises (D-169), and a let unit is its tenant's, so the guard left its door alone and no casing card or lock ever opened. The store is now the nearest warehouse with nothing to let.
* **The raid asked for less than its door needs** (raid run 2): the store's high-security lock opens only to a Pro Pick Set with Professional Tools (Thief level 8), but the job hired from Thief level 3 and its text offered a crowbar, which the game does not have. The job now asks for Professional Tools and says what the door takes.
* **The bonded store is The Yard by night** (found reading the code before raid run 1): every furnished Ironhaven warehouse keeps a fence who trades until 03:00, when the building counts as open and its public floor cannot be searched. The job says to go after three.
* **The civic stalls were never built** (civic run 2): the spot search tried only the bare street height Lost Cities reports, while the walkable surface can be one or two blocks up. It now allows what the street people's spot search allows; run 3 built them.
* **Order card layout** (the captures): the status line ran under the Track chip at 1280x720, and the Crew line was cut off; the line wraps short of the chip, and the card hides the supplier price, which the Supplier button shows. A first fix shortened the line itself, which took away the price the button reads and hid the button; the 1080p GUI 3 capture showed it, and it was put right before anything was pushed.
* **Notices stacked down the middle of a short window** (civic run 3's picture at 1280x720): at most a third of the scaled height is now notices (two at 240, three from 360); the rest wait their turn.
* **A tracked order could not be stopped** (raid run 3's pictures): the panel sent the player to the Places book, which stops only places. The terminal's Track chip now reads Stop while that order is tracked, the panel says so, and its details start with a capital.

* **No gun learned from a TaCZ blueprint ever showed on the table** (gunsmith runs 2 to 4, found only through a real client): TaCZ Blueprints 0.1.3 records a gun learned from its blueprint item under the item's `GunId` (`tacz:glock_17`), but its table screen shows a gun only when `tacz:gun/glock_17` is learned, so even TaCZ's own Glock recipe was hidden from a player holding the knowledge. The GameTests had passed because they call the table's craft directly. Core now records every learned gun under both spellings, the tick after a blueprint is used and at login, and sends the addon's own sync packet (`BlueprintGate.learnBothSpellings`, GameTested; run 6 shows `[tacz:glock_17, tacz:gun/glock_17]`).
* **Nothing told a new Gunsmith to read the kit's blueprint** (gunsmith run 2 opened the table to an empty pistol tab): registration now says so in chat and in a notice, and so does opening the table while an unread blueprint is in the pack.
* **A TaCZ gun on an order card drew as a missing texture** (gunsmith run 6's pictures: the magenta and black square for "2 x Registered pistol"): a TaCZ gun item has no model without its `GunId`, so the terminal now draws a gun requirement with the first gun the order accepts.
* Scenario faults, not game bugs: the scenario read the screen's selection before the blueprint was read (run 2), the placing click opened the new bench before its parts were given (run 3), and the generic spot search found no room for the terminal beside the bench and table (run 5).

Still open from these runs: TaCZ's table lists three recipes all named "Glock 17" (TaCZ's own iron recipe, which Core refuses at the craft for a kit gun, and Core's registered and unmarked kit recipes); only the ingredient panel tells them apart.
