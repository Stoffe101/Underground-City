# Sprint report, October 16, 2026: the first playable experience

Branch `feature/professions-vehicles-firearms-sprint`, draft PR [#21](https://github.com/Stoffe101/Underground-City/pull/21) into `integration`. Nothing was merged. Everything below was built and tested in Claude's cloud container and on GitHub-hosted runners, never on the owner's computer.

**Status vocabulary** (as in the [October 15 report](sprint-2026-10-15.md)): *implemented and tested* = code exists and an automated test that actually executes it passed (the kind of test is named); *scripted-player check* = an actual Minecraft client was driven with genuine keyboard and mouse events by a script, which proves the chain from input to world and says nothing about feel; *partial*, *planned*, *not tested* mean what they say. **No human has played any of this.** A scripted-player run is never described as a person playing.

## A. Executive summary

**The short version.** The repository can now be launched with one Gradle command that loads the complete development pack (section C), a new world has a populated **Founders Row** with a first-login screen, a registrar, an employer for each career and an Operations Terminal that opens when an employer is spoken to (section H), a registered Gunsmith can go from a blueprint to a real TaCZ gun and ammunition to a paid contract (sections F and G), and **a scripted player, using real keyboard and mouse events on a real client, placed and drove an Immersive Vehicles Quad along a generated road and across a generated bridge, sailed a Modern Life Motorboat under another bridge, fired a TaCZ pistol, got searched at a checkpoint post and ran the Technician's trace through its success, wrong-answer, abandon and timeout paths** (sections E, I, K). **No human has played any of it**, Windows and IntelliJ are untested, and the world is still three Lost Cities shells joined by roads: there are no inhabited cities, no police response, no balanced economy and no second player on this pack.

**What I would tell the owner first.**

1. *Yes, one command launches it* (`.\gradlew.bat runClient`, JDK 17), verified on Linux in the cloud and on a hosted runner, **not** on Windows or from IntelliJ. Section C has the exact steps and what to expect.
2. *The vehicles work in the sense that a script can get in, move, steer and brake on the real roads and bridges.* Survival fuel for a car is untested (creative-mode vehicles are fuel-free), and the Quad's steering is touchy: the script needed a tap-and-wait controller to keep it on the road at all.
3. *A real finding from this sprint:* employers wander to the village bell by day and would tell you to "come into the office" from across the lane. They now say so and walk back to their desk.
4. *The test machinery is itself a deliverable and a liability:* ten scripted-player scenarios and their helpers now exist (`Scenarios.java`, `Playtest.java`, `RoadDriver.java`, `RoadWalker.java`); they are slow, container-specific and were repeatedly wrong before they were right (sections E and K say how).
5. *The next blocker is not code:* a human needs to play the first thirty minutes on a real machine (section M, P0).

**What was hard, and what it means.** About seventeen scripted drives failed before one passed, and the reasons are worth keeping:

- *Controls travel to the server and come back late.* Immersive Vehicles sends the keys to the server and shows what the server's physics did. In this container (a software-rendered client sharing four cores with the integrated server) the answer arrives a good half second late, and the Quad turns about 50 degrees in a second from a few ticks of steering. A controller that reacts to what it sees swung from lock to lock; the one that finally worked **taps a steering key for two ticks every 16 ticks and waits**, reading the vehicle's own orientation (found by trying the four local axes against the direction it actually moves in; the model's front is local +Z).
- *A vehicle stops dead at the edge of the loaded world.* The server was minutes behind its ticks whenever many chunks were generated, and a vehicle that outruns the chunks the server has sent stops and may be dropped from its rider. The scripts therefore generate the road's chunks first, hold them loaded, wait, cap the frame rate and the view distance, and cruise at 6 (car) and 9 (boat) blocks per second. A real machine with a GPU will not behave like this, which is also why these numbers say nothing about performance.
- *The screenshot taken "under the bridge" was a stale frame* (the render target of a software client can be seconds old); moving scenes are now photographed after the next completely drawn frame. The Motorboat's own camera tilts the view up under acceleration, so some boat frames show only sky; the scripts measure that tilt and correct for it, and the frames to trust are named in section K.
- *A real finding for players:* Immersive Vehicles' keyboard steering is incremental (2 degrees of wheel per tick held, 4 per tick of return), so holding A or D is not "turning", it is "turning more every tick". The Quad is skittish at speed over the road's slab ramps; a human will learn it, but the first-minutes experience of a vehicle on these roads is untested.

**Not covered:** survival fuel use, saving and reloading a vehicle with its fuel and cargo, a passenger, a second player, a crash, a fuel station, running a checkpoint with a car (Immersive Vehicles' cargo is not a Container, so a car's trunk is not searched at all), and the Motorboat's hold while it is moving (its cargo round trip is a GameTest without a world).


## F. Firearms and the Gunsmith loop

Firearms come from **real TaCZ 1.1.8-hotfix2** and **TaCZ Blueprints 0.1.3** (pinned, runtime-only, never committed). Core owns the *rules around* a gun (who may carry it, what a checkpoint does with it, what a contract pays for it); TaCZ owns the gun, its ammunition and its workstations. Core's law data classifies a TaCZ gun by registry id and the `GunId` NBT key, so a Glock 17 is a *licensed sidearm* (legal in Blackwater, restricted elsewhere) and a gun that never went through a licence is contraband where the zone says so. Legal gunsmithing and the underground errands for the same guns stay different contracts with different buyers (section G and the [careers design](../design/legal-and-underground-careers.md)).

| Check | Kind | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration grants the Gunsmith kit (a Gun Smith Table, an Ammo Workbench, the Glock 17 blueprint, 16 iron, 10 copper, 2 gunpowder) and a second registration grants nothing | GameTest, real TaCZ jars | passes (64 of 64 in the firearms profile) |
| Blueprint from the hand fills `GunUnlocks`; the real table recipe crafts the Glock 17; the real Ammo Workbench recipe crafts 50 rounds of 9mm; the Valewood co-op buys the crafted gun and pays through Q Shop | GameTest, real TaCZ and TaCZ Blueprints | passes, also on hosted runners |
| A held Glock 17 with an empty magazine: the player presses **R** to reload from the inventory (magazine 0 to 16 rounds, carried 9mm 50 to 33), then presses the **left mouse button** at a zombie (target health 20.0 to 13.1, magazine 16 to 15); the HUD reads 000 / 0050, then 017 / 0033 | scripted player: real X11 key and mouse events into a real client | passes (`gun` scenario; the two screenshots were viewed) |
| A registered Gunsmith carrying an unlicensed pistol is searched at a checkpoint post and the pistol is confiscated | scripted player, search forced with `/uc_dev search` | passes (section I) |

**What this does not show.** It does not show that firing *feels* right (recoil, sound, the target dying from a magazine), that the table's own screen is pleasant (nobody opened it with a mouse; the recipe was crafted through the menu's server-side `doCraft`), that a client without the blueprint cannot craft (the gate is client-side in TaCZ Blueprints 0.1.3, so it is a convenience and not an authority), that a police response follows a shot (there is none), or that shotgun and SMG parts exist in the supply chain (only the pistol path is built end to end). TaCZ's own licence terms (GPL-3.0 code, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 gun pack) matter for any later distribution and are recorded in the [mod audit](../technical/mod-audit.md).

**The Gunsmith loop (blueprint to contract).** Registration grants the kit. Using the blueprint in the hand fills the player's `GunUnlocks`; the Gun Smith Table's own recipe for the Glock 17 consumes 16 iron ingots (exactly the kit's iron) and **drops the finished gun at the player's feet**; ammunition is a **different workstation** in TaCZ, the Ammo Workbench (an item named `workbench_a` that carries `BlockId`), whose recipe turns 10 copper ingots and 2 gunpowder into 50 rounds of 9mm (the Gun Smith Table refuses ammunition recipes; the first version of this loop assumed it did and the GameTest caught it); the Valewood co-op's legal `licensed_sidearm` contract then buys the **crafted** gun and pays through Q Shop while the smith keeps the ammunition. **What a person is meant to feel in the first minutes** (design intent, not a measurement): arrive at the Row gate, the registrar greets a newcomer and opens the city-then-profession screen, the welcome pack arrives, the employer offers an order with a visible shopping list on screen, delivering it pays and teaches the next step. No human has done this; see section L for the scripted-player evidence.

*People.* Nine named villagers (a registrar, four employers, three residents, a guard) plus Morgan. They are persistent and invulnerable, each has a bed or a workstation or both so that vanilla routines apply (sleep in the bed, work at the workstation, meet at the bell), and what they say is data. The registrar opens the first-login screen for a newcomer and, for a registered citizen, names their next step with their employer's name and a rough direction. An employer opens the Operations Terminal bound to their office for a citizen of their own career; to anyone else they say what they do and name that citizen's own employer. Residents and the guard talk and hint at real mechanics (apartment locks and lockpicks, buyer trust, heat). `/uc_district repopulate founders_row` restores a lost citizen. **What this is not:** there are no schedules beyond the vanilla ones, no memory of the player, no relationships, no per-NPC quest state, and no off-screen population; villagers can still breed when they have beds and food and wander toward the bell. Employers do not pay anyone; contracts and credits still go through the terminal and Q Shop.

*Safe placement (unchanged rules).* The build validates every block and policy before touching the world, refuses a lot that contains any block entity, saves the replaced blocks for `/uc_district undo founders_row confirm`, and a failed build puts everything back. Citizens are discarded by undo. A lot may carry `requires_mod` operations (the TaCZ table is skipped without TaCZ) and a district may be `repeatable` (the checkpoint post).

*Dev worlds.* In a brand-new development world the launch builds the Row on level, dry ground near the spawn point, found from the chunk generator's own heights (so no chunk is generated to look for it; outside every city circle; at most 3 blocks of relief) and moves the world spawn to the gate ([D-064](decisions.md)). In any other world an operator runs `/uc_district build founders_row` at the spot they choose. **Not done:** a fixed lot inside a city of the generated world, and doors, furniture or decoration beyond what is listed.


## I. Crime and the underground in the world

The rules did not change (D-051: evidence, never omniscience); what changed is that they now have a **place and an actor**.

| What | State |
| --- | --- |
| Checkpoint **posts** | `/uc_checkpoints place` builds the data-defined `checkpoint_post` (a 9 x 9 paved lot, a booth, a barrel, a blast furnace, lanterns and one guard) beside the road at each of the six zones, on the road's left-hand side and clear of its deck, parapet and clearing band (the placement maths is a pure function with a GameTest over the real corridor geometry). Each post is its own reversible district (`/uc_checkpoints remove confirm`), so the six posts of a world never overlap and none blocks the road. The zones are 48-block circles, so a traveller who leaves the road still walks around the zone: that is the documented smuggler's trail, not a bug |
| The **guard** | a named, invulnerable villager (an armorer at a blast furnace) who turns to the traveller and voices the search. **The zone, not the guard, decides** who is searched (the seeded die over the city's law, the traveller's heat and skills); the guard does not patrol, chase, arrest or see anything |
| **Vehicle cargo** | a rider's vehicle that has a `Container` hold (the Motorboat's 27 slots, a chest boat, a minecart with a chest) is searched together with the rider and contraband in it is confiscated; a parked vehicle is not searched. GameTested. **Immersive Vehicles' cargo is not a `Container` and is not covered**, so a car's trunk is not searched yet |
| **Boat customs** | none. A boat that sails through a zone is searched like any rider; there is no customs building and no water-side post |
| **Buyer trust and finite demand** | as before: per-player trust (maximum 5) gates the larger contracts, buyer demand is finite and replenishes only with active play |
| **Consequences** | confiscation and a fine from the wallet; heat that decays with active play. No arrest, no jail, no raid, no police response to a shot being fired |

## N. New ideas

Not decisions; offered for the owner to accept, change or ignore.

- **Make the gate guard a source of leads, not only a voice.** A guard who names which goods the city is cracking down on this week gives the smuggler a read on risk and the legal trader a reason to check the law page before carrying a permit good (the data already has per-city law).
- **A ledger board in the registrar's office** showing, for each employer, how many orders are open and how much buyer demand is left. It teaches finite demand by looking, and it is the natural first thing a second player checks.
- **Let the Gunsmith's trade have a second room.** A licensed range behind the counter (a few target blocks, no live NPCs) where a crafted gun can be tried before it is sold; it turns "the contract paid" into "I made something that works".
- **Make the registrar the hub of the first 30 minutes.** After a profession is chosen, the registrar names the employer; after the first delivery, the employer names a second employer in another career. This is a data change to the dialogue, and it nudges two humans to cover each other's careers, which fits the private co-op rule.
- **A road milestone every 250 blocks** carrying the distance and the next gate. It costs one block and gives the long Valewood-Blackwater deck a sense of progress, and it is where a roadside encounter could one day stand.
- **Two ways to cross the Valewood-Blackwater ridge.** The deck climbs to y 122; a lower service road for carts and a high viaduct for walkers would turn the one 4-minute walk into a small choice. Seed-dependent: check against the real terrain before drawing anything.
