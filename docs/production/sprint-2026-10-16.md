## C. One command: `gradlew runClient` with the complete development pack

**Can a normal user launch the complete pack with one Gradle command? Yes on the platform that was tested, with two honest caveats: it has been run on Linux in the cloud and on GitHub's hosted Linux runner, not on Windows 11 and not from IntelliJ IDEA, because no such machine is available to the cloud session.** The Windows path is the same Gradle build (`gradlew.bat`, no shell scripts, no hard-coded paths), but it is unverified. [dev-client.md](../technical/dev-client.md) is the full guide.

What was fixed to make this possible: a committed Gradle wrapper (8.14.3, checksum pinned; the validator and the hosted wrapper-validation job accept exactly that jar), a root build that forwards to `rpg-core`, Modern Life's configuration read (`-Dproduction=true`, because Forge only throws when `FMLEnvironment.production` is false), Immersive Vehicles' content packs (they are found by scanning the game directory's `mods` folder, so the manifest places them there), the Lost Cities profile and the Q Shop currency templates (staged only when absent), and the ignored-directory bug that hid the generated world package from the first hosted run (`.gitignore` matched `run-*/`).

**Evidence.** `./gradlew runClient -PucLaunchSmoke=true` in the cloud container: nine mods with versions, 470 and 137 content-pack items, seed 5908, an Operations Terminal opens its screen. The same command on a GitHub-hosted runner under Xvfb with software OpenGL (`dev-client-smoke.yml`, job "runClient launches the complete pack and reaches a terminal", about 4 minutes from a cold cache): `[UC-LAUNCH] RESULT pass`. `./gradlew runServer -PucBootSmoke=true` boots the same pack (hosted job "runServer boots the complete pack"). **Not covered:** Windows, IntelliJ's own Gradle import, a real GPU, two players on this pack, and driving or sailing (section E).

The first hosted attempt failed, and the hosted log is why this is believable rather than assumed: the generated world package directory was named `run-config`, which `.gitignore` (`run-*/`) silently ignored, so the CI checkout had no Lost Cities configuration; it is now `game-config` and the diagnostic step prints Gradle's reason into the job log.

## G. The four professions

Each career now has a **person to work for in the world** (an employer in Founders Row whose office is bound to a terminal), a **starter kit** granted once at registration (`data/underground_city/starter_kits/<career>.json`), and an **on-screen tracker** for the accepted order. Everything else (skill trees, 19 contracts, underground branches) is as in the [October 15 report](sprint-2026-10-15.md). The registrar tells a registered citizen the next step: see your employer, then spend the first skill point, then "keep going".

| Career | Employer in Founders Row | Starter kit | What a session can do today | What is still missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Farmer / Vintner | **Odette Marsh**, farm co-op (composter, barrels, hay, a bed) | wooden hoe, 12 wheat seeds, 4 bread | take a greenhouse or wholesale order at the terminal, deliver, learn Restricted Cultivation, raise Sable Leaf under lamps in the Row's irrigated greenhouse, sell it underground | vintner content; harvest-to-terminal needs the player to carry goods by hand; no growing schedule beyond vanilla |
| Smith / Engineer | **Brannock Voss**, foundry (smithing table, anvil, furnace) | stone pickaxe, 8 coal, 4 bread | structural steel and guard plating orders; the underground branch takes unstamped plate | no forge-specific workstation or recipe of ours: the foundry uses vanilla blocks |
| Gunsmith / Arms Dealer | **Ines Calloway**, gunsmith counter (grindstone, anvil, a real TaCZ gun smith table when TaCZ is loaded) | bow, 16 arrows, 4 bread, **and with the firearm mods:** a gun smith table, an ammo workbench, a pistol blueprint (Glock 17), 16 iron, 10 copper, 2 gunpowder | **the loop below** | the blueprint gate is client-side in TaCZ Blueprints 0.1.3; no gun ever becomes police evidence outside a checkpoint search; shotgun and SMG parts supply is not built |
| Hacker / Technician | **Kit Arden**, data office with the secured terminal | 16 redstone, 8 copper, 4 bread | the Operations Terminal for legal automation orders; **the trace** at the secured terminal (section I of the October 15 report), with the kit consumed and a lock-down on failure | no hacking target other than the one secured terminal; the sequence reaches the client, so it is a memory check, not a security feature |

**The Gunsmith loop (blueprint to contract).** Registration grants the kit. Using the blueprint in the hand fills the player's `GunUnlocks`; the Gun Smith Table's own recipe for the Glock 17 consumes 16 iron ingots (exactly the kit's iron) and **drops the finished gun at the player's feet**; ammunition is a **different workstation** in TaCZ, the Ammo Workbench (an item named `workbench_a` that carries `BlockId`), whose recipe turns 10 copper ingots and 2 gunpowder into 50 rounds of 9mm (the Gun Smith Table refuses ammunition recipes; the first version of this loop assumed it did and the GameTest caught it); the Valewood co-op's legal `licensed_sidearm` contract then buys the **crafted** gun and pays through Q Shop while the smith keeps the ammunition. Every server-side step runs in the GameTest `gunsmithKitBlueprintTableAndAmmoCraftARealLicensedSidearmThatTheCoopBuys` against the real TaCZ and TaCZ Blueprints jars. It does **not** prove the table's screen, that firing the crafted gun works (section F has the scripted-player check for the pistol), or that a client without the blueprint cannot craft (it can, see section J).

**What a person is meant to feel in the first minutes** (design intent, not a measurement): arrive at the Row gate, the registrar greets a newcomer and opens the city-then-profession screen, the welcome pack arrives, the employer offers an order with a visible shopping list on screen, delivering it pays and teaches the next step. No human has done this; see section L for the scripted-player evidence.

## H. The living world: Founders Row

Founders Row is no longer a block of stalls: it is a lot (25 x 41 blocks, up to 12 high) with a paved lane from the stalls in the north to a gate in the south, built from `scripts/generate_district.py` into `data/underground_city/districts/founders_row.json` (270 operations, nine citizens, bounded and validated like every district).

| Place | What is there |
| --- | --- |
| North row | the supply stall (Operations Terminal and **Morgan**, the supply clerk), a records stall with three registered Household Safes, the dark-stone data office (the Secured Data Terminal, a terminal and the Technician's employer **Kit Arden**), and a glass greenhouse with irrigated soil for the controlled crop |
| West side | **Ines Calloway**'s gunsmith counter (grindstone, anvil, a real TaCZ gun smith table when TaCZ is loaded, a bed), **Brannock Voss**'s foundry (smithing table, anvil, furnace), and two cottages (**Tomas Reyes**, a haulier; **Mira Voss**, a retired courier) each with a bed and an apartment-grade registered safe |
| East side | **Odette Marsh**'s farm co-op (composter, barrels, hay, a bed), **Ada Brightwater**'s cottage (a market gardener, bed and safe), a storehouse with two registered safes of two security grades, and the registry where **Imani Okafor**, the registrar, works beside an Operations Terminal |
| The lane | a village bell on a pedestal at the middle (the meeting point villagers gather at), lanterns, and a gate with **Sergeant Halloran** at his post |

*People.* Nine named villagers (a registrar, four employers, three residents, a guard) plus Morgan. They are persistent and invulnerable, each has a bed or a workstation or both so that vanilla routines apply (sleep in the bed, work at the workstation, meet at the bell), and what they say is data. The registrar opens the first-login screen for a newcomer and, for a registered citizen, names their next step with their employer's name and a rough direction. An employer opens the Operations Terminal bound to their office for a citizen of their own career; to anyone else they say what they do and name that citizen's own employer. Residents and the guard talk and hint at real mechanics (apartment locks and lockpicks, buyer trust, heat). `/uc_district repopulate founders_row` restores a lost citizen. **What this is not:** there are no schedules beyond the vanilla ones, no memory of the player, no relationships, no per-NPC quest state, and no off-screen population; villagers can still breed when they have beds and food and wander toward the bell. Employers do not pay anyone; contracts and credits still go through the terminal and Q Shop.

*Safe placement (unchanged rules).* The build validates every block and policy before touching the world, refuses a lot that contains any block entity, saves the replaced blocks for `/uc_district undo founders_row confirm`, and a failed build puts everything back. Citizens are discarded by undo. A lot may carry `requires_mod` operations (the TaCZ table is skipped without TaCZ) and a district may be `repeatable` (the checkpoint post).

*Dev worlds.* In a brand-new development world the launch builds the Row on level, dry ground near the spawn point, found from the chunk generator's own heights (so no chunk is generated to look for it; outside every city circle; at most 3 blocks of relief) and moves the world spawn to the gate ([D-064](decisions.md)). In any other world an operator runs `/uc_district build founders_row` at the spot they choose. **Not done:** a fixed lot inside a city of the generated world, and doors, furniture or decoration beyond what is listed.


## I. Crime and the underground in the world

The rules did not change (D-051: evidence, never omniscience); what changed is that they now have a **place and an actor**.

| What | State |
| --- | --- |
| Checkpoint **posts** | `/uc_checkpoints place` builds the data-defined `checkpoint_post` (a 9 x 9 paved lot, a booth, a barrel, a blast furnace, lanterns and one guard) beside the road at each of the six zones, on the road's left-hand side and clear of its deck, parapet and clearing band (the placement maths is a pure function with a GameTest over the real corridor geometry). Each post is its own reversible district (`/uc_checkpoints remove confirm`), so the six posts of a world never overlap and none blocks the road. The zones are 48-block circles, so a traveller who leaves the road still walks around the zone: that is the documented smuggler's trail, not a bug |
| The **guard** | a named, invulnerable villager (an armorer at a blast furnace) who turns to the traveller and voices the search. **The zone, not the guard, decides** who is searched (the seeded die over the city's law, the traveller's heat and skills); the guard does not patrol, chase, arrest or see anything |
| **Vehicle cargo** | a rider's vehicle that has a `Container` hold (the Motorboat's 27 slots, a chest boat, a minecart with a chest) is searched together with the rider and contraband in it is confiscated; a parked vehicle is not searched. GameTested. **Immersive Vehicles' cargo is not a `Container` and is not covered**, so a car's trunk is not searched yet |
| **Boat customs** | none. A boat that sails through a zone is searched like any rider; there is no customs building and no water-side post |
| **Buyer trust and finite demand** | as before: per-player trust (maximum 5) gates the larger contracts, buyer demand is finite and replenishes only with active play |
| **Consequences** | confiscation and a fine from the wallet; heat that decays with active play. No arrest, no jail, no raid, no police response to a shot being fired |
| **A burglary location** | the Row's records stall holds three registered Household Safes and its storehouse two more of different security grades, and every cottage has an apartment-grade safe (eight in all). They are the lock-and-key mechanic from before, now standing in a populated place |

*Visible from the road:* a person walking the road meets a lit post with a named guard at each gate zone only after an operator has run `/uc_checkpoints place`. In a development world the launch builds Founders Row but **not** the posts; the first post is a one-line command away.

*What the scripted player checked* (real client, real keys, `posts` scenario): the six posts were built next to the corridor, the player was moved to the first post, a registered Gunsmith carrying an unlicensed pistol was searched by that post's zone and the pistol was confiscated (the search was forced with `/uc_dev search`, which runs the real search and confiscation code but skips the die, so the chance itself is not exercised there). The screenshots are described in section K; the first attempt photographed the sky before the chunks had meshed and was corrected before it counted.

## N. New ideas

Not decisions; offered for the owner to accept, change or ignore.

- **Make the gate guard a source of leads, not only a voice.** A guard who names which goods the city is cracking down on this week gives the smuggler a read on risk and the legal trader a reason to check the law page before carrying a permit good (the data already has per-city law).
- **A ledger board in the registrar's office** showing, for each employer, how many orders are open and how much buyer demand is left. It teaches finite demand by looking, and it is the natural first thing a second player checks.
- **Let the Gunsmith's trade have a second room.** A licensed range behind the counter (a few target blocks, no live NPCs) where a crafted gun can be tried before it is sold; it turns "the contract paid" into "I made something that works".
- **Make the registrar the hub of the first 30 minutes.** After a profession is chosen, the registrar names the employer; after the first delivery, the employer names a second employer in another career. This is a data change to the dialogue, and it nudges two humans to cover each other's careers, which fits the private co-op rule.
- **A road milestone every 250 blocks** carrying the distance and the next gate. It costs one block and gives the long Valewood-Blackwater deck a sense of progress, and it is where a roadside encounter could one day stand.
- **Two ways to cross the Valewood-Blackwater ridge.** The deck climbs to y 122; a lower service road for carts and a high viaduct for walkers would turn the one 4-minute walk into a small choice. Seed-dependent: check against the real terrain before drawing anything.
