# Core gameplay loops

## Loop A: peaceful livelihood

1. Obtain a contract or identify market demand.
2. Acquire legitimate materials and operate a profession-specific workstation.
3. Produce quality-dependent goods using learned recipes.
4. Transport/deliver the goods, receive payment and career XP.
5. Pay costs, invest in equipment/property/skills, improve your city's supply and fiscal position.
6. Reinvest in a more ambitious supply chain, new customer, or community project.

### Example: vintner

Own grape plot → manage soil/crops → press/ferment → sell to inn/town buyer → receive funds and reputation → unlock cellar/estate contracts. Let product quality, seasons and contract requirements matter without punishing a casual schedule.

## Loop B: industry and research

Discover or purchase a schematic/blueprint → validate career level + skill specialization + research → source parts → use a suitable workbench → produce/repair or trade → unlock a higher tier. Smithing/engineering and firearms manufacturing share materials but have distinct licensed and black-market customers.

## Loop C: exploration

Hear about a location from NPC dialogue or rumor → prepare supplies and transportation → explore roads/buildings/ruins → obtain per-player Lootr rewards or finite shared resources → return safely → trade, research, or fulfill objectives. Meaningful loot must have scarcity and believable origin.

## Loop D: crime and consequences

Choose a risky opportunity → arrange contact and route → evade or confront faction/security response → sell/complete job → face evidence-based law, heat, reputation changes or confiscation. Legitimate alternatives must remain viable.

## Loop E: city influence and story

Help residents, solve regional problems, complete public works, learn secrets and make decisions → raise trust/influence and change city policy/territory → unlock new districts, infrastructure, characters and story beats.

## Sessions and anti-grind rules

- A 30-minute session should contain achievable work and a visible reward.
- XP is weighted toward varied meaningful work and completed contracts; repeated craft/recycle loops are capped or ineligible.
- Offline time must not impose taxes, debt spirals, or unstoppable wars.
- City changes should offer visible feedback (new checkpoint, renovated building, patrol, NPC dialogue, supply/prices).
- Neither player needs to stop progressing while the other is absent.
